Who I am

I work where people, systems, and organizations meet.

I began in the mid-1990s as a web designer who did everything—graphic design, front-end coding, and back-end coding. Over time, I moved toward information architecture, enterprise UX, business analysis, and customer experience.

“It’s just what I asked for, but not what I needed.” The sentence good UX prevents

The through line

Complex work, made understandable.

I have often been hired for one position and then steered toward another. A front-end role became information architecture. UX work expanded into business analysis and project leadership. Customer research grew into the design of the systems that collect and distribute insight.

The titles changed because the problem required it. The underlying work did not: understand how people really operate, expose the structure inside the complexity, and help different disciplines arrive at the same useful solution.

How I work

Three habits I bring to a problem.

I am most useful when a project has many stakeholders, dense information, technical constraints, and no single person holding the whole picture.

01

Find the real problem

Requests are evidence, not answers. I ask what people are trying to accomplish, what gets in their way, and what would make the work meaningfully better.

02

Translate across disciplines

I am comfortable moving among users, operations, design, engineering, and leadership—making each group’s constraints legible to the others.

03

Stay close to implementation

My technical background helps me design work that can survive contact with data, business rules, edge cases, and production reality.
